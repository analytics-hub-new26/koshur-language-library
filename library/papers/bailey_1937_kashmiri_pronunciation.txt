T. Grahame Bailey (School of Oriental and African Studies, London)

1. THE VOWEL SYSTEM
Kashmiri boasts an unusually complex vowel system comprising 16 distinct vowel phonemes:
- Front unrounded: i, iː, e, eː
- Central unrounded: ɨ, ɨː, ə, əː (rare in South Asia, requiring distinctive macrons)
- Back rounded: u, uː, o, oː, ɔ, ɔː
- Low central: a, aː

2. MATRA VOWELS (FINAL WEAK VOWELS)
A defining discovery of Bailey and Grierson is the presence of short, unaccented final vowels termed 'mātrā-vowels' (i-mātrā, u-mātrā). While scarcely audible in rapid speech, these vowels exert profound morphophonemic effects on preceding consonants and vowels through regressive assimilation (umlaut / epenthesis).

3. PALATALIZATION (MATSARYAR)
Every consonant in Kashmiri, with few exceptions, has a palatalized counterpart:
    non-palatalized: gur (horse)
    palatalized: gər' (horses)
The dental series (t, th, d, n) and affricates (ts, tsh, ch, chh, z) undergo precise phonetic transformations in plural and past-tense environments.
