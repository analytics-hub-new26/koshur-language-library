================================================================================
DARDIC AND THE LINGUISTIC POSITION OF KASHMIRI
Georg Morgenstierne (University of Oslo)
Acta Orientalia / Indo-Iranian Journal
================================================================================

1. CLASSIFICATION OF DARDIC
Morgenstierne conclusively demonstrated that the Dardic languages (including Kashmiri, Shina, Khowar, Kohistani, Pashai, and Kalasha) do not form a third independent branch of Indo-Iranian alongside Indo-Aryan and Iranian, but represent an archaic subgroup of Indo-Aryan that diverged early and remained insulated in the Hindu Kush and Himalayan valleys.

2. ARCHAIC CONSONANT RETENTION IN KISHTWARI
While Valley Kashmiri has simplified many Sanskrit conjuncts (e.g., tr- > tr / t):
    Sanskrit 'bhrātā' -> Kashmiri 'bōi'
Kishtwari (the southeastern dialect of Kashmiri) retains ancient Dardic consonant clusters:
    Sanskrit 'trayo' -> Kishtwari 'trei'
    Sanskrit 'kroḍa' -> Kishtwari 'kroel'

3. LEXICAL COGNATES
A comparative analysis across Kashmiri, Shina, and Khowar reveals a deeply shared pastoral, alpine, and domestic vocabulary distinct from Plains Indo-Aryan (Hindi/Punjabi):
    Snow: Kashmiri 'sheen', Shina 'hin'
    Cold: Kashmiri 'tur', Khowar 'tur'
    Water/Spring: Kashmiri 'naag', Shina 'naag'
